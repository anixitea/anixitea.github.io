GlassBook

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 21, 2026

GlassBook is an audiobook player for Android made by one person, theglassmaker. This page explains what the app does with your information.

The short version: GlassBook has no server of its own and no analytics or ads. Your books and what you listen to stay on your phone, or in your own Google account if you choose to sign in. I never receive any of it.

What stays on your phone

Your library, where you are in each book, your bookmarks, your collections, your Want to Listen list, your listening history and the listening time it shows, and your settings are all stored on your device. Uninstalling the app deletes them. GlassBook plays your audiobook files from wherever they are on your phone, in the folders you choose, and only reads those folders.

If you give a book or a collection a cover from your photos, the app keeps a small square copy of the picture you chose. It only reads the one picture you pick, with Android's photo picker, and has no access to the rest of your photos.

If the app crashes or stops responding, a report is saved on your phone. It's only sent anywhere if you choose to share it yourself from Settings, using whichever app you pick.

A backup file is saved wherever you choose to save it.

Who the app talks to

  • Audible's public catalog. To find a book's cover and details (by itself when you add a book, or when you use Match online), and for Discover, Search and store pages, the app asks Audible's public catalog. It sends a book's title and author, your search words, or a book's catalog number. Discover asks for books like the few you're listening to, by their title or catalog number; your library as a whole isn't sent.
  • Audnexus. For a matched book's description and chapters, the app asks Audnexus, a public service for audiobook details, with the book's catalog number.
  • Open Library. For what readers are reading lately, on Discover, and some covers.
  • Image servers. Covers in Discover and Search load from the addresses the catalogs give for them.
  • GitHub. The app checks GitHub for new versions of GlassBook and downloads updates from there.
  • Google, only if you sign in. See below.

Like any website, these services can see your IP address when the app asks them for something.

Sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. It keeps your library the same on every phone you use, and lets you keep your audiobooks in your own Google Drive.

What GlassBook asks Google for

Two permissions:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.appdata, shown by Google as "See, create, and delete its own configuration data in your Google Drive". It lets GlassBook keep one file of its own, glassbook-library.json, in a hidden app folder in your Drive. Only GlassBook can open that folder.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file, shown by Google as "See, edit, create, and delete only the specific Google Drive files you use with this app". It lets GlassBook keep your books in a GlassBook folder in your Drive, if you turn on Keep books in Drive. GlassBook can only see the files it created there. It can't see, open or change any of your other files.

What's stored there

The library file holds, for each book in your library, its title, author, length, chapters, description and the details Match online found, where you are in it, your bookmarks, its collection, and when you added it, finished it or took it off Continue listening. It also holds your collections' covers, your Want to Listen list and the books you marked Not interested in Discover, books you removed (so your other phones remove them too), and the app's settings. Your listening history and listening time stay on each device.

With Keep books in Drive on, the GlassBook folder holds a folder for each book, with its audio files and its cover. You can see these in Google Drive like any of your files, and they count against your Google storage.

All of this is in your Google account, not on a server of mine.

Your name, email address and photo

The app reads these from your Google account so Settings can show which account you're signed in with. They stay on your device.

How it's used

  • Only to keep your library in sync between your phones, and to store and play your books.
  • It isn't sold, shared or given to anyone, and it isn't used for advertising or to train AI models.
  • Nobody reads it. I have no way to: it's in your account, and only the app on your own devices opens it.

GlassBook's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Signing out and deleting

  • Sign out in Settings to remove GlassBook's access to your Google account. Your library stays on the device, and what's in your Drive stays there for the next time you sign in.
  • To delete your books from Drive, delete the GlassBook folder in Google Drive.
  • To delete the library file, open Google Drive on the web, go to Settings, then Manage apps, find GlassBook, and choose Delete hidden app data.
  • You can also remove GlassBook's access at any time from myaccount.google.com/permissions.

Children

GlassBook isn't made for children under 13, and it doesn't knowingly collect information from them.

Changes and contact

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new date.
